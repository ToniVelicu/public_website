About

I am a post-doctoral researcher at the Soziologisches Institut, University of Zurich. My work is held together by a single question: what happens when people deviate from norms — and what does that reveal about the norms themselves?

In my research, I explore this through two lenses. The first is science: how academic reward systems shape researcher behavior, what drives questionable research practices, and who gets left behind by the rules of the game. The second is intimate life: how norms around love, partnership, and family are shifting — who challenges them, how, and at what cost. Both strands draw on the same sociological instinct — that deviance tells us more about a social order than conformity ever could.

Empirically, I work primarily with the Zurich Survey of Academics (ZSoA), combining large-scale surveys, survey experiments, bibliometric methods, and qualitative approaches informed by grounded theory. I received my doctorate from the University of Zurich in 2024 (magna cum laude), with a thesis on intersectionality and misconduct in science.

Research

What I work on

Academic reward systems

How do publication norms and incentive structures shape researcher behavior? Do playing by the rules actually pay off?

Research integrity & misconduct

What drives questionable research practices, and how do scientists justify them? Evidence from large-scale surveys in the DACH region.

Gender inequality in science

From the gender productivity gap to homogamous partnerships in academia — structural explanations for who gets ahead and who doesn't.

Publication bias

Do scientists systematically favor statistically significant results? Experimental and quasi-experimental evidence from peer review.
